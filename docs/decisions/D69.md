# D69 — A single-valued relation stays keyed on its subject, and a date's subject is the thing that is due

**Date.** 2026-11-13. **Issue.** [#188](https://github.com/ensera-ai/taisce/issues/188).

**Why it was open.** A relation that holds one value at a time is keyed on subject and predicate.
That is what lets a new value close the old one. It is wrong when the subject is not one thing: two
deadlines both stated of "the bidder" would have the later close the earlier, and a true fact would
be given an end it never had. Nobody had counted how often that happens.

**What was counted.** In the requirements-document measurement
([the write-up](../39-requirements-document-measurement.md)), five runs, one document:

- **Dates: no wrong closure.** With the thing that is due as the subject — which the prompt now
  asks for (D68) — two deadlines are `due_on(Questions from bidders, …)` and `due_on(Proposals, …)`.
  Two subjects, two facts.
- **Roles: one wrong closure in every run.** `has_role(Dervla Quinn, procurement lead)` was closed by
  `has_role(Dervla Quinn, chair of the panel)`, stated five sections later. Both are true.

**Decided.**
- **The key stays subject and predicate.** Supersession is what makes "I moved to Cork" end "I live
  in Dublin", and it depends on exactly this key.
- **For a date, the subject is the thing that is due or scheduled**, by guidance in the prompt. That
  removes the case the finding was written about, on the evidence above.
- **`has_role` stays single-valued.** The vocabulary was written for conversation, where the common
  statement about a role is a change of one: "I am now the team lead" should end "I am an engineer".
  Made many-valued, every such change would leave the old role current. The measured failure is the
  other case — two roles held at once — and it is real, and it is the rarer one in the text this
  vocabulary was written for.
- **A closed fact is not lost.** It is kept with its interval, the fact that replaced it and the turn
  that caused it, so a wrong closure can be found and counted, as the measurement's harness does.

**Rejected: a qualifier on single-valued facts** — a role *in what*, a status *of what* — in the key
and in the exclusion constraint. It is the complete answer, and a schema change through
supersession, on a count of one closure in one authored document. It is what to build if real
documents show the same closure at a rate that matters.

**Rejected: making `has_role` many-valued.** Described above. It trades a false end for a missing
one, in the more common case.

**Rejected: refusing a second value when both come from one document.** A document describes one
state of the world, so two values for one subject in it conflict and do not succeed each other. The
store does not know which turns are one document. Giving it that is a column with one reader.

**What it does not cover.**
- **The closure that was measured.** A person with two roles at once still loses the first to the
  second. An operator loading organisational documents can add a many-valued relation for positions
  held.
- **Other single-valued relations.** `has_status`, `located_in` and `instance_of` were not exercised
  by the document.
- **A model that names the party as the subject of a date anyway.** The guidance is wording, and
  wording is not a bound: the count has to be repeated when the model changes.

**What would reopen it.** The same count on real documents, which the harness now makes cheap.

**Undo cost.** Nothing structural was changed.

**Impact.**
- *What is remembered.* Two deadlines in one document no longer risk closing each other. Two roles
  held at once still do.
