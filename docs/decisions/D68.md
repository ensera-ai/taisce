# D68 — A deadline, a schedule and an obligation hold now, and the rule that refuses what does not stays

**Date.** 2026-11-13. **Issue.** [#187](https://github.com/ensera-ai/taisce/issues/187).

**Why it was open.** Extraction admits only relations that hold as the message is written, and
refuses the rest as `not_current`. The rule is right: its alternative is a false fact, not a missing
one. It was written for conversation, and the worry was what it costs a document made of
obligations and dates — a tender, a contract, a specification.

**What was measured.** One requirements document written for the purpose, 38 listed items,
`deepseek-flash`, 2026-11-13 ([the write-up](../39-requirements-document-measurement.md)). Before
any change: 20 of 38 kept. All four dates were refused as `not_current`, because the day had not
come. A section of five "shall" requirements produced nothing at all, not even a refusal.

**Decided.**
- **The present-tense rule stays.** A relation that has not started or has ended is still refused.
- **A deadline or a schedule is a present fact about a date.** "Proposals are due on 4 April" says
  what the date is, and that holds before the day arrives. It is reported as present, with the
  thing that is due or scheduled as its subject and not the party it concerns.
- **An obligation is a present fact about who is bound.** "The contractor shall encrypt data at
  rest" states a duty that binds now, whether or not it has been done.
- **Both are wording in the prompt file, held by the regression corpus.** Two cases were added for
  dates and one that a plan to move is still not a residence. After the change the corpus passed 22
  of 22, and the document kept 29 to 34 of 38 over four runs: every date, and 16 of 17 obligations
  in the run read in full.

**Rejected: admitting `future` or `past` as facts.** Recall answers what holds. A relation that has
not happened, returned as a fact, is the false fact the rule exists to prevent. A deadline is not
that: what is future is the day, and what is stated is the date.

**Rejected: a deterministic parser for requirements, writing authored records.** No model judgement
on text that has structure is the right instinct, and a tender's structure is its author's: numbered
clauses in one, a table in the next. A parser would be per document family, and the measurement
shows the prompt was the smaller defect.

**Rejected: leaving the rule and documenting that such documents are a poor fit.** It was the
position before the measurement. The measurement showed the loss came from two missing sentences of
guidance and not from the rule.

**What it does not cover.**
- **Criteria and weights.** "The technical criterion has a weight of fifty percent" has no relation
  to be stored as: 1 of 4 kept, before and after. An operator who loads such documents adds the
  relation to their vocabulary.
- **An obligation's content stated as a fact.** One run stored `located_in(a support desk, Ireland)`
  beside the obligation to provide one. The desk does not exist yet. One in 36 facts, read by hand.
- **Any document but one, and any model but one.** The document was written by the person who
  scored it.
- **A question that names nothing**, which the walk cannot start.

**Proof.** `TestTheExtractionCorpus`, cases *a deadline that has not come*, *a scheduled start* and
*a plan is still not the thing planned*, against a real model. `TestWhatARequirementsDocumentKeeps`
is the measurement, re-runnable, and asserts only that it ran.

**Undo cost.** Low: two paragraphs of a prompt file. The extraction identity changes with them, as
on any prompt edit.

**Impact.**
- *What is remembered.* A document's timetable and its duties are stored where they were refused.
