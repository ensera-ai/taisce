# D50 — A credential may end on its own, and an operator's does unless somebody says otherwise

**Date.** 2026-11-12. **Issue.** [#223](https://github.com/ensera-ai/taisce/issues/223).

**Why it was open.** A credential lived until somebody revoked it, and revoking needs somebody to
know it should end. The first operator credential — minted at install, pasted somewhere, able to
mint a key for any project — was the instance's most powerful secret for as long as the instance
existed. And an operator deciding what was safe to revoke had nothing to go on: the list said what
existed and not what was in use.

**What is protected, and from whom.** The management surface and each project's memory, from
whoever comes to hold a copy of a token nobody is thinking about any more: in a shell history, a
ticket, a decommissioned machine's environment.

**Decided.**
- **A credential may carry the moment it stops resolving**, a nullable `expires_at`.
- **It is enforced in the query that finds a credential, beside revocation, by the database's
  clock.** An expired credential is a row the query does not return. Every door gives it the answer
  a stranger gets, with nothing to remember at any of them, and no replica's clock is consulted.
- **The lifetime is given as a length and the database fixes the moment.** A process whose clock
  runs ahead cannot mint a credential that is already dead.
- **An operator credential ends after ninety days unless whoever mints it says otherwise.** A
  permanent one is permitted and has to be asked for by name, `-no-expiry`; a lifetime of zero is
  refused rather than read as "never", because zero is what a flag holds when a variable was empty.
- **A project credential and an observer credential get a date only when asked.**
- **Existing credentials are not touched.** Their `expires_at` is null and they end by revocation.
- **Every newly minted token says when it ends and how to mint its replacement**, at the one moment
  the operator is certainly reading.
- **The credential list says when each credential last appears in the audit ledger**, read when the
  list is asked for. Nothing is written when a credential is used.

**Why ninety days.** It is a choice and not a measurement. Long enough that replacing the credential
is a quarterly task, rather than a weekly one that gets automated by storing the credential
somewhere worse; short enough that a copy left behind is dead within a season. It costs nothing that
cannot be got back: minting needs the administrative connection, not a credential, so expiry locks
out nobody who could have minted it.

**Rejected: a default lifetime for project credentials.** It is the consistent rule. A project
credential is held by an application, and a date nobody chose is the day that application stops
authenticating in production, with a refusal that is — deliberately — indistinguishable from a
mistyped token. A default that causes an outage for everyone who did not read about it is not a
secure default; it is a deferred one. The date is offered and the list shows which credentials have
none.

**Rejected: a last-used column on the credential, written when it resolves.** It makes the list a
single-table read. It is also a write on every request, on the read path, to the registry — which
the serving role can only read, by a grant that is the reason the registry is a boundary. The ledger
already records the principal of every operation and is indexed by principal and time.

**Rejected: checking the expiry in the callers.** A check after resolution is one each door has to
remember, and the door that forgot would be open to an expired credential with nothing failing.

**Rejected: stamping a date on existing credentials in the migration.** It would end the indefinite
life of every credential already issued. It would also choose, for every deployment at once and
without telling anybody, the day their applications stop working.

**Rejected here, and still the direction: no stored key at all.** A workload presenting an identity
its platform already issued, exchanged at the moment of use, removes the thing that can be stolen
rather than dating it. It is a second resolution path with a key set to trust and nothing to offer a
single-machine deployment, and it is a decision of its own.

**What it does not cover.**
- **Nothing warns before a credential ends.** The token says when, once, at issuance, and the list
  shows the date. A credential that ends unnoticed is refused as a stranger, and the holder is told
  nothing.
- **"Last acted" is the newest entry the ledger holds.** A credential absent from it has never
  appeared in the ledger as it stands. It counts a recorded refusal as an appearance, and it cannot
  see a credential that resolved and did nothing the ledger records.
- **The ops centre issues project credentials with no date.** The CLI and the management API take a
  lifetime; the form does not ask for one.
- **A credential issued before this has no date** and is exactly as permanent as it was.

**Proof.** `TestACredentialPastItsExpiryIsRefusedExactlyAsARevokedOneIs` at the store's three doors,
with a credential that really expires. `TestAnExpiredCredentialIsAStrangerAtBothDoors` walks every
memory and management route and requires the answer a token nobody issued gets, body included.
`TestAnOperatorCredentialEndsUnlessSomebodySaysOtherwise` holds which kinds get a date unasked.
`TestTheCredentialListSaysWhenEachEndsAndWhenItLastActed` holds the list.
`TestAnOperatorCredentialIsPermanentOnlyWhenAskedForByName` holds the flags.

**Undo cost.** Low. The column is nullable and additive; dropping the predicate restores the old
behaviour and no row has to change. What cannot be undone is a credential that already expired and
was replaced — which is the intended effect.

**Impact.**
- *Security.* The most powerful credential on a new instance stops working on its own. An operator
  can see which credentials nothing is using.
- *Operations.* An operator credential minted from this release on has to be replaced within ninety
  days, or minted with another lifetime. Anything that authenticates to the management surface with
  one — a script, a scraper not yet moved to an observer credential — stops at that date.
